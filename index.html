Action Network Data Study

Will they actually get your order right?

We priced 1,651 drive-thru visits across 10 chains like a sportsbook would. Some windows are heavy favorites. One is a genuine longshot.

Every drive-thru order is a small wager. You say the words, you hand over the money, you pull away, and only a mile later, at a red light, do you find out whether the bag contains what you asked for or a burger wearing the exact condiment you declined.

So here at The Action Network, we did what we do best: we turned it into odds. Think of it as a sportsbook line for your lunch. Is your regular spot a heavy chalk favorite to nail the order, or are you laying money on a coin flip every time you say "no pickles"?

We ran the numbers on 1,651 unannounced drive-thru visits across 10 major chains. Here are the fast food chains with the best (and worst) odds.

Fun Findings

  • 11% of drive-thru orders come back wrong.
  • Taco Bell is the longest shot on the board, botching 15% of orders.
  • McDonald's and Chick-fil-A are co-favorites, both at just 7% chance of an order mishap.
  • Speed and accuracy trade off against each other, hard. Comparing chains, the slower the drive-thru, the more accurate it is. Taco Bell is the fastest chain in the study and the least accurate. Chick-fil-A is the slowest and tied for the most accurate.
  • Among burger chains, McDonald's is the safest bet by a mile (7%) and Wendy's is the riskiest (13%), nearly double the error rate.
  • The riskiest bet in fast food is asking for no ice. It was the single most-cited failure in the entire study, unfulfilled 27.7% of the time.
  • A slow wait in your own line means the opposite. Within a single visit, accurate orders were handed over 50 seconds faster than inaccurate ones. Chains that budget more time per car make fewer mistakes, but a car stuck at the window is usually a mistake being fixed. If you have been sitting there an unreasonably long time, start bracing.

Ten straight visits anywhere is close to a coin flip even at the best chain in America.

How To Shorten Your Own Odds

Three factors moved accuracy meaningfully, and two of them are just about whether the speaker box works.

  • +15%accuracy when the speaker was clear and understandable
  • +12%accuracy when the speaker was loud enough to hear
  • +7%accuracy when the shopper didn't have to repeat the order
  • +18%accuracy when the shopper described the service as friendly

Translation: if you can barely make out the voice in the box, you have already lost value on this bet. That's your cue to check the bag before you pull out, or to park and go inside. Same with a curt, rushed exchange. Friendliness turns out to be a leading indicator of competence, not a separate quality.

One more line worth watching. Only 4% of visits involved AI taking the order, just 58 shops, far too small to bet on. But in those visits accuracy hit 95%, against a study average of 89%. It's an early number on a market that hasn't really opened yet, and if it holds up as voice AI scales, the entire board above gets redrawn.
